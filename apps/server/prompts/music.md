Music: on this machine Studio Engine makes music with its music script, node "{{musicScript}}", only when the job asks for it explicitly; a video that nobody asked music for ships without, and nothing is made or fetched for it. This replaces the Music row of the skill's §0 defaults table ("Only files the user supplied … Never download or generate music unasked; with no music file, ship without and say so."): follow these steps instead. The rest of that row (several music files: take the one that fits the pacing and note the other) and upstream's "Music and sound effects" rules still hold.
- Every call takes `"<edit dir>"`: the absolute path of ./edit/, always in double quotes. Run each call as a Bash command of its own, in the foreground, and read the one JSON line it prints: {"ok":true,…}, or {"ok":false,"code":…,"message":…} with a non-zero exit. The script is the only thing that writes edit/music/ and edit/preview/music.mp3: never write, trim, normalise or re-encode them yourself.
- The script reads the ElevenLabs key itself. Never make or fetch music any other way (no ElevenLabs call of your own, no other music tool or skill, no download), and never read or print the key.
- Where the music comes from. Ask the brief first, then its Series section, then its Client style section; the first one that settles it decides:
  - A music file the brief lists as music: use it. Run `node "{{musicScript}}" supplied "<edit dir>" "<the file, as the brief lists it>"`.
  - A series episode: the kit's music, from edit/series/kit/assets/audio/. Run `node "{{musicScript}}" series "<edit dir>" "edit/series/kit/assets/audio/<file>"` and keep the kit's timing and levels for it.
  - A rule that says no music (in the brief, from the owner's note or the client's instructions, or in the series or the client's style): run `node "{{musicScript}}" none "<edit dir>" "<whose rule, in one plain sentence>"`, for example "The client's style says no music.", and use no music. A rule that keeps music out of only some parts is not this: make the music and keep it out of those parts with the volume lane.
{{> music-songs-sources}}
  - The job asks for music to be made or added: make the music, as in "Making it" below. Music is asked for only when one of these says so, never because a video could use it:
    - The brief's Decisions have the line "Music: asked for, made by the editor" (edit/brief.json: music.wanted is true), or its summary, its must-include list or the owner's note (word for word in the brief) ask for music in so many words: "add music", "with a soundtrack", "intense music with drops", "תוסיפו מוזיקה". A brief written before this rule has no music.wanted, and its line "Music: not asked for" cannot know: read the owner's note and the summary yourself, whatever the line says. Asked in the brief, it beats a no-music rule of the series or the client's style, since the brief is asked first.
    - The Series or the Client style section has a rule that tells to have music in the videos ("Do: add a soundtrack with drops on the cuts"). A line that only describes the taste of music, or what the reference videos had ("Music: upbeat electronic", "no vocals"), is not an ask. Neither is the wish for a good video, its tone, its platform or its footage.
    - The owner asked for it in Review (a version they made there is in edit/music/music.json): keep it, as "No music asked for" below says.
  - Nothing above asks for music (the brief's line "Music: not asked for" only says that the instructions did not; the series and the client's style are read too): there is no music. See "No music asked for" below.
{{> music-songs}}
- No music asked for. Make nothing: no make, no song, no download, no ElevenLabs credit. If edit/music/music.json already has music picked (a version made in an earlier run of this job, or one the owner made in Review), keep it as it is and do nothing more here. Otherwise, once the cut's shape is fixed, write edit/music/plan.json from that shape exactly as "Making it" says (it is what Make music in Review makes from, if the owner asks for some), then run `node "{{musicScript}}" none "<edit dir>" "Music was not asked for." --plan`. The script records "no music" with that plan and puts silence of the plan's length at edit/preview/music.mp3. Build the composition with its music element and its volume lane exactly as if the make had worked (the script's silence plays now, and a version the owner makes in Review drops straight in), and say in project.md's Assumptions line: "Music was not asked for, so none was made." If the script says the plan does not fit, fix plan.json and run it again; if it still fails, run it once without --plan and leave the music element out.
- Making it. Once the cut's shape is fixed (you know the time of every cut), and before you build the composition, write edit/music/plan.json from that shape. This is the shape of a 39 s cut with its drop at 18.0 s:
  {"lengthMs": 39000, "styles": ["intense cinematic electronic", "120 BPM", "big drop", "instrumental"], "avoid": ["vocals", "singing", "lyrics"], "sections": [{"name": "Build", "ms": 10000, "styles": ["sparse pulse", "filtered synths", "rising tension"], "avoid": ["drums"]}, {"name": "Rise", "ms": 8000, "styles": ["snare roll", "riser", "building energy"], "avoid": ["kick drum", "sub bass", "full drums"]}, {"name": "Drop", "ms": 6000, "styles": ["big drop", "kick drum", "sub bass", "full drums"], "avoid": []}, {"name": "Golden", "ms": 10000, "styles": ["euphoric", "wide synth chords", "driving drums"], "avoid": []}, {"name": "Logo", "ms": 5000, "styles": ["final impact hit", "short tail"], "avoid": []}], "describe": "Intense cinematic electronic, 120 BPM. Builds to a big drop at 0:18 and lands on the logo at 0:34."}
  - lengthMs is the composition's exact duration in ms. The sections are the cut's own parts (the build, the drop, the speech, the end card), each starting and ending exactly on a cut, in whole ms and 3000 to 120000 ms long (join a shorter part to its neighbour), and together they add up to lengthMs exactly.
  - Each section's name is one or two plain words, like "Build", "Rise", "Drop", "Golden" or "Logo": the script sends it to ElevenLabs as "[Name]", and any other words there can be sung. Never a sentence, a direction or lyrics. styles and avoid hold only short style tags like the ones above, never sentences or directions.
  - styles come from the brief's tone and pacing and the Music line of the Client style, with the tempo when the cut sits on a beat grid. Always instrumental: "instrumental" in styles, and vocals, singing and lyrics in avoid. Under speech, ask for something sparse that leaves room for the voice.
  - A drop has to land as a change: the section right before it has the drop's weight in its avoid ("kick drum", "sub bass", "full drums"), and the drop's own section asks for it in its styles, as above.
  - describe is one plain line for the owner, at most 160 characters: what it sounds like and where its big moment lands, as above.
  - Then run `node "{{musicScript}}" make "<edit dir>"` with a Bash timeout of 600000 ms (it takes a minute or two). It makes edit/music/v1.mp3 and puts it at edit/preview/music.mp3.
  - If edit/music/music.json already has a version from an earlier run of this job and the cut's shape has not changed, keep it and don't make another. One make that works is enough unless its drop misses (the drop check below): never make another for taste (the owner can in Review). If you change the cut's length or shape after the make, update plan.json and make it again.
  - If a make fails, its line says why. A plan that doesn't fit: fix plan.json and run it again. ElevenLabs down or slow (exit 3), or another error: run it again. ElevenLabs saying no (exit 2: credits, access or the key): don't. Never run make more than twice in this run. When there is still no music, the script leaves edit/preview/music.mp3 as silence of the plan's length: skip the fitting below, but keep the music element and its volume lane in the composition exactly as if the make had worked, so a version the owner makes later in Review drops straight in (leave the element out only if music.mp3 is missing). Say in project.md's Assumptions line that the music could not be made and why.
- Fitting the cut to it. ElevenLabs keeps the section lengths, but its hits can land a little off them, so measure the track, don't assume: `npx hyperframes audio "<edit dir>/preview/music.mp3" --only beats,downbeats,bpm,sections --json` (edit-video's Phase 8.4). Find where its big moments really land: the drop (the measured section change nearest the plan's drop) and the last hit.
  - The drop check. If the plan has a drop and the measured one landed more than one beat from it (60 / BPM seconds: 0.5 s at 120 BPM), run `node "{{musicScript}}" make "<edit dir>" --again` once, with the same timeout, and measure the new take the same way. It counts toward the two makes in this run: never a third, and if this run has made twice already, keep the take you have. The make picks its new version; if the earlier take's drop was closer, put that one back with `node "{{musicScript}}" pick "<edit dir>" <its id>` (the id its make printed, e.g. v1). If this make fails, the earlier take stays.
  - Then move the cuts onto the nearest beats of the take you kept (about one frame early reads as on the beat) and the drop's cut onto its measured drop, keeping the total length. Write in project.md what you measured (the plan's drop, where each take's drop landed and which take you kept) and what you moved.
- Playing it. Whatever its source, the composition plays the music from edit/preview/music.mp3 (src="music.mp3"), never from a copy: Review swaps that file when the owner picks another version. Use one `<audio id="music">`, from 0 for the composition's whole duration unless the series kit times it otherwise, with a volume lane in data-automation. Music the script made comes at −20 LUFS integrated, every version alike, and the lane's values are linear gain (1 is the file as it is, 2 is about +6 dB, 3.98 is +12 dB, the most a lane takes). Where the music is the only sound (no speech), the lane may lift it by up to +12 dB: about 2 (+6 dB) puts it near −14 LUFS, the delivery level. Under speech, duck it to 12 to 15 dB below its music-only level, and ramp it out before a stinger or the end card's words. Set its level only with the lane (a lane replaces data-volume), never by changing music.mp3: every version the owner can pick has the same loudness, so one lane fits them all.
