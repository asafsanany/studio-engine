- Songs. The client or the owner may ask for a song under the video. Fill music.song, music.original and music.wantsTrending from what the instructions say; when nobody asks for a song they are null, null and false. music.note keeps only what the instructions say about how the music is used (“start at the drop”), or null: never where a song came from.
  - A screenshot of a reel or a TikTok whose audio label names a song is instructions, not a reference: it says which song to cut to. Read the label word for word. On Instagram it is the line with a music note under the account name, “Artist · Title”. On TikTok it is the scrolling line next to the spinning record, usually “Title - Artist”. Put the title in song.title and the artist in song.artist exactly as written, Hebrew included: never translate, shorten, correct or look up a name. Put the screenshot’s file name in song.fromFile.
  - “sped up”, “slowed” or “remix” in the label or in the chat is song.version, and “original” only when it says so; otherwise song.version is null.
  - “Original audio · [account]”, “original sound” or the same words in Hebrew name no song. Set music.original to { "by": "[account]" } with the account as written on the label, and song to null. Never guess a song from it, from the footage or from the account.
  - A label that is cut off (it ends in “…”): write what is visible and say in assumptions that the label was cut off. Never complete it from memory.
  - A link that the client or the owner ties to the music (YouTube, music.youtube.com, an Instagram reel, TikTok, Spotify, Apple Music) goes word for word in song.link. When the link is all there is, song.title and song.artist stay null: never invent a name for it. A song named in words goes in song.title and song.artist, with song.link null.
  - A line of a form, a template or a document that gives the music as a name (“Music: [title] - [artist]”, “מוזיקה: [title] - [artist]”) names a song when no file in the folder has that name: put it in song.title and song.artist as written, and leave music.file null. music.file is only for a file that is in the folder (role music): a name with no file behind it never goes there.
  - “A trending song”, “שיר טרנדי”, “something viral”, “a song that’s trending” with no song named: music.wantsTrending is true and song is null.
  - A song someone asked for is not assumed. Conflicts between two songs follow step 4: The owner’s note first, and the screenshots they attached to it count as their note (they are in edit/brief/from-you/ and are listed in step 1 when there are any), then the newest message. A music file in the folder (role music) still wins over any song: then song stays null, music.wantsTrending is false, and the conflict goes in assumptions.
